01 / Research question

Keeping the map in place
as the viewer moves.

How can we estimate a phone’s position and orientation in a robot’s map, then maintain alignment while the user walks and turns?

From readings to spatial understanding

Wi-Fi coverage is difficult to interpret spatially from individual signal readings. We will build and evaluate a smartphone system that makes robot-collected measurements visible while users explore a room, aligned with their position and viewing direction.

An integration and evaluation challenge

Our course contribution is a Go2-to-iPhone Wi-Fi visualization system built from existing localization and tracking components. The central challenges are tracking drift, localization latency, and consistent alignment between the phone and the robot map.

02 / System approach

Survey once. Explore on foot.

The robot gathers the measurements. Visual localization establishes the shared reference, and ARKit tracks the phone as the user moves.

01 / SURVEY

Build the room map

Use Go2 to collect geometry, reference imagery, and location-tagged Wi-Fi readings for one room and one access point.

OUTPUT · Spatial map + surveyed signal samples
02 / ALIGN

Locate the phone

Register visual references to the robot map. Use image-based localization to estimate phone pose in that shared coordinate system.

OUTPUT · Phone-to-map alignment
03 / EXPLORE

Walk through the signal

Combine the alignment with ARKit tracking and display surveyed Wi-Fi estimates as the user walks and turns.

OUTPUT · Spatially aligned iPhone visualization
Data source

Displayed signal values will come from the robot’s survey. A phone photograph helps with localization; it does not measure the Wi-Fi strength at a pictured location.

Minimum deliverable

A working iPhone walkthrough, evaluated for alignment accuracy, tracking stability, and display responsiveness.

Single stretch goal

Localize a newly captured photo, then let a user select a mapped point to query its estimated Wi-Fi strength.

Outside the initial scope

Multi-room operation, live Wi-Fi updates, and full volumetric radio mapping.

Contribution

Integrating and evaluating existing mapping, localization, and tracking components—not training a new localization model.

03 / Evaluation plan

Does the signal stay where it belongs?

We will check estimates at independently measured positions and evaluate a short walkthrough. These are planned measurements; results are not yet available.

04 / Feasibility & next steps

Test the hardest assumption first.

The largest risk is unreliable visual alignment between phone images and the robot map. Our first milestone is a focused feasibility test.

First two-week milestone

  1. Confirm compute availability, Wi-Fi measurement hardware, and compatibility of the scan and reference poses.
  2. Test phone-image localization against room references registered to the robot map.
  3. Check localization estimates at independently measured positions.
  4. Test marker-initialized ARKit alignment during a short walk.

Resources & fallback

Available: Go2 access and publicly available localization code.

Needed: an ARKit-compatible iPhone, a Mac for development, Wi-Fi measurement hardware, and a computer for localization. Compute availability and scan/reference-pose compatibility need confirmation.

Fallback: surveyed visual markers will initialize or restore ARKit alignment if image-based alignment proves unreliable, preserving the walking visualization and its evaluation.
